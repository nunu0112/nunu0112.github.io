Research

Four projects, one question: how do we build models of psychological processes whose parameters can actually be believed?

My projects differ in method — Bayesian cognitive models, joint neural–behavioral models, tree ensembles, causal forests — but share a structure. Each begins with a process that cannot be observed directly, specifies a model that would generate the observed data if that process were real, and then spends as much effort establishing that the model's estimates are trustworthy as on the estimates themselves. Each page below gives the background, the methods in technical detail, and the results.

Modeling Choice and Response Time in Value-Based Decision-Making

Reinforcement learning models of the Iowa Gambling Task use choices alone and recover individual-level parameters poorly. I replaced the softmax choice rule with a racing diffusion process, derived the joint likelihood of choice and response time, and implemented it in Stan — then showed by simulation that all seven person-level parameters recover accurately, where the standard benchmark model does not.

Joint Modeling of Single-Trial Behavior and fMRI Activity

Model-based cognitive neuroscience usually estimates cognitive parameters first and correlates them with brain activity afterwards, discarding uncertainty and letting information flow only one way. I extended a lasso factor-analytic neural diffusion decision model to a rewarded recognition-memory task so that single-trial decision parameters and regional brain responses are estimated in one pass.

Interpretable Machine Learning for Dyadic Prediction

Fertility desires are negotiated by two people, which breaks the independence that ordinary cross-validation assumes. I built a couple-grouped, leakage-controlled prediction pipeline with out-of-fold SHAP attribution, and used it to ask whether knowing someone's partner adds predictive information beyond knowing the person — a question about redundancy that a regression coefficient cannot answer.

Heterogeneous Treatment Effects of COVID-19 Events on Well-Being

A published study had asked whether extraverts suffered more under social distancing, using interactions specified in advance. I re-asked the question inside a potential-outcomes framework with generalized random forests, letting the data locate the heterogeneity, and gated every interpretation behind a formal calibration test.
