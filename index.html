About

I am a postgraduate researcher at the Psychometric & Computational Modeling Lab (PI: Inhan Kang), Department of Psychology, Yonsei University. I hold an M.S. in Psychology from Yonsei University, a B.A. in Psychology, and a B.S. in Computer Science and Engineering from Seoul National University.

My research aims to quantify and interpret the underlying processes that give rise to psychological phenomena. I work broadly across computational modeling (reinforcement learning, sequential sampling models), joint behavioral–neural modeling, and machine learning, selecting methods based on the mechanisms I am trying to uncover in a given psychological process. I am particularly interested in integrating behavioral data with neural data to build more complete, mechanistically grounded accounts of cognition and behavior. More broadly, I am motivated by the question of how psychological science itself can be made more rigorous and cumulative, particularly through advances in model validation and interpretable methods.

Research

Modeling choice and response time together

Development and Validation of a Reinforcement Learning Sequential Sampling Model for Addictive Behavior · with Inhan Kang · Submitted, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review

Behavioral addiction reflects a persistent preference for immediate rewards over long-term outcomes — a breakdown in value-based decision-making. I integrated a reinforcement learning model for the Iowa Gambling Task (IGT) with the evidence-accumulation structure of sequential sampling models, producing a joint model of choices and response times. A simulation study shows accurate recovery of all seven individual-level parameters, and the model reproduces card-wise choice trajectories, win-stay/lose-shift patterns, and empirical RT distributions in data from 20 older adults — matching or outperforming several choice-only RL models.

Linking neural and behavioral data

Joint Modeling of Behavioral and Neural Data via Lasso Factor-Analytic Neural Diffusion Decision Models: Reward-Modulated Recognition Memory · with Seoyoung Choi, Sungwoo Ahn, Sanghoon Han, Inhan Kang · Data analysis in progress

A model-based cognitive neuroscience project linking neural activity to decision-making parameters in reward-modulated recognition memory, using Lasso factor-analytic neural diffusion decision models to jointly account for behavioral and neural data.

Interpretable ML for dyadic data

Predicting Fertility Desires Among Engaged Couples: An Interpretable Machine Learning Approach Using Dyadic Data · with Hyoungbin Moon, Young-Hoon Kim, Inhan Kang · Manuscript in preparation

Applies interpretable machine learning methods to dyadic survey data from engaged couples to identify predictors of fertility desire.
