About

I am a Ph.D. applicant in the Department of Psychology at Yonsei University, working with Prof. Inhan Kang in Quantitative Psychology. My research combines reinforcement learning (RL) and sequential sampling models (SSMs) to build unified, computational accounts of how people make decisions — using both choice and response-time data to recover psychologically meaningful, individual-level parameters.

I hold a B.S. in Computer Science and Engineering and a B.A. in Psychology from Seoul National University, and an M.S. in Quantitative Psychology from Yonsei University. This background lets me move fluidly between formal computational modeling (Bayesian estimation, Stan, hBayesDM) and substantive psychological questions about value-based decision-making, addiction, and memory.

Research

Development and Validation of a Reinforcement Learning Sequential Sampling Model for Addictive Behavior

with Inhan Kang · Submitted, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review

Behavioral addiction reflects a persistent preference for immediate rewards over long-term outcomes — a breakdown in value-based decision-making. I integrated a reinforcement learning model for the Iowa Gambling Task (IGT) with the evidence-accumulation structure of sequential sampling models, producing a joint model of choices and response times. A simulation study shows accurate recovery of all seven individual-level parameters, and the model reproduces card-wise choice trajectories, win-stay/lose-shift patterns, and empirical RT distributions in data from 20 older adults — matching or outperforming several choice-only RL models.

Joint Modeling of Behavioral and Neural Data via Lasso Factor-Analytic Neural Diffusion Decision Models: Reward-Modulated Recognition Memory

with Seoyoung Choi, Sungwoo Ahn, Sanghoon Han, Inhan Kang · Data analysis in progress

A model-based cognitive neuroscience project linking neural activity to decision-making parameters in reward-modulated recognition memory, using Lasso factor-analytic neural diffusion decision models to jointly account for behavioral and neural data.

Predicting Fertility Desires Among Engaged Couples: An Interpretable Machine Learning Approach Using Dyadic Data

with Hyoungbin Moon, Young-Hoon Kim, Inhan Kang · Manuscript in preparation

Applies interpretable machine learning methods to dyadic survey data from engaged couples to identify predictors of fertility desire.
